Built on my own research on agent reliability and silent failure. A paper I have under review is titled Silent Failure in Automated Model Adaptation, and Attest Fleet is that thesis made runnable.

The problem

An agent reports refund issued. The payment gateway quietly left it pending. The run counts as a success, the board stays green, and the customer never gets their money. Research finds this silent failure is up to 48% of failures in single-control tasks and 75.8% on AppWorld. It is the failure nobody sees.

Why today's tools miss it

Observability tools grade the agent with another model as judge. That LLM judge catches silent failure at only ~0.65 AUROC, barely better than a coin flip, because a confident wrong claim reads as success to a model too. Attest does not ask a model. It reads the system of record and compares, deterministically.

Who it benefits

  • The night-shift support lead who inherits a fleet she did not build and cannot read the code of. At 2am the board says every ticket closed green. She is the one a customer calls when it did not. Attest gives her the one thing she never had: a list of the runs that lied, before the complaints arrive.
  • The solo operator at a small lender or clinic who bought agents from a vendor, has no ML team, and is personally accountable for every refund and record change.
  • Customers who would otherwise be silently wronged: refunds lost, tickets closed unfixed.
  • Auditors and risk teams who need evidence a claimed outcome actually happened, not a log saying an agent said so.

The vision: agent fleets you can trust in production, because every claimed outcome is verified against reality, risky actions pause for a human, and the whole run is auditable evidence instead of faith in a self-report.
